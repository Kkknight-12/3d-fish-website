A closer look.

A freshwater world

Koi are ornamental carp, selectively bred in Japan for their colors and patterns. Today, they are kept in ponds and water gardens worldwide.

Smithsonian National Zoo ↗

Forage. Flow. Repeat.

Koi are omnivores that eat plants, algae and aquatic insects. A wave through the body and tail drives swimming; fins help with stability and steering. This long-finned illustration exaggerates the movement and is not a biological simulation.

Diet · Smithsonian ↗Movement · NOAA ↗

Decades of life

San Diego Zoo lists a typical lifespan of 25–35 years with proper care.

San Diego Zoo ↗

Tiny beginnings

Koi spawn through external fertilization. Their sticky eggs attach to aquatic vegetation. The eggs-to-fry diagram is schematic and not to scale.

Spawning · SRAC / UF IFAS ↗
How many eggs? A closer look

Tempero and colleagues (2006) examined 44 ready-to-spawn feral females in New Zealand. Estimates ranged from 29,800 to 771,000 developing eggs. These are study-specific egg counts, not surviving young or a universal number per spawn.
